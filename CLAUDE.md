# CLAUDE.md

## Repository rules

- This is the only repository to write to for Brightvein.
- These sibling repositories are read-only references, set in the same world. Reuse their assets, mechanics and code when useful, but never commit to them:
  - `chriskizer91-ops/airship-game-in-aethermoor-` (the skies: flight, guns, raiders, the six ships, the map)
  - `chriskizer91-ops/envoi-on-the-longest-night` (the looks and portraits Brightvein's visiting cast wear)
  - `chriskizer91-ops/moon-faire` (the looks of the four Moon Faire performers and their festival airship)
- When copying assets or code from a sibling repo, note the source repo and path in the commit message.
- Brightvein is hundreds of years after Chris's other games (Chris, October 18). It reuses their art and borrows their world, but its people are new: the visiting cast wear the looks and portraits of Envoi's and the Moon Faire's characters under new names and new stories (`CAST_NAMES` in `src/claim/story.js`). Never use the old names (Io, Sol, Halcyon, Quill, Brann, Tock, the Magpie) or those games' plots in what players see. The cast's ids in the code (`io`, `sol`, ...) are only the names of the art. Envoi's retired lore stays out: the Drowned Mother, Old Snuff, the mandrakes and Mother's Hollow.

## Project

- *Brightvein*: a sunstone claim on a plateau under the Thinning, in Aethermoor (Chris's D&D world). Fallout Shelter's staffed rooms and people with stats, Clash of Clans' base building and defence, and the airship game's skies.
- Its own story (`docs/story.md`): the claim, the Wildlight, Baron Tallow and his claim-jumpers are Brightvein's. Visitors arrive in its chapters (Tamsin and Brisa on the Nightjar, Barnabus and Nib, Torvald, the Moon Faire's four, Dame Seraphine of the Thinwatch) and can stay as special crew.
- Design notes: the claude.ai project docs `brightvein-design.md` (the whole game) and `brightvein-keep-design.md` (the Keep and study). They aren't in the repo.
- Look: HD-2D. A lit 3D diorama with pixel-art people as camera-facing sprites. People are hand-pixeled in code (`src/claim/pixel.js`): 48 × 64 frames, 10 columns (stand, walk × 4, work × 3, idle × 2: a breath and a blink) × 3 rows (facing you, facing away, facing left; right is mirrored). `drawPortrait` draws a bust for anyone without a painted portrait. `python3 test/people.py --check` draws every look and must pass.
- Desktop first (Chris, October 18), but phones must keep working: touch controls and the Light detail setting.
- Chris tries things on his phone. Every step should end with something he can open there. Write anything Chris reads in plain words.
- When new art is needed, write the picture prompts as markdown files in `docs/art-requests/` for Chris to generate.

## Code

- `src/claim/`: the claim (town, Keep, mine, people, Wildlight, loot, UI). One shared scope: its files are joined in the order of `src/claim/order.json` into one module. Add a file there to add it to the build.
  - `folk.js`: everyone's portrait and look, picture first. Settlers come off this roll so their sprite and portrait match. `node tools/portrait-prompts.mjs` writes `docs/art-requests/03-portraits.md` from it.
  - `cast.js`: the visiting cast's looks. `story.js`: chapters, scenes, visitors, the journal and codex (`docs/story.md`). `dock.js`: the Sky Dock (the bridge and gantry, mooring the flagship, `openSky`, coming home, `dockLink`: what the skies ask of the claim). `yard.js`: the fleet's claim side: the Dock's panel (Ship, Crew, Raids tabs), the crew picked from the claim's people (`yardSeat`, `yardCrewFor`), repairs finished on claim time (`yardTick`), wanted captains (`yardNewDay`), the raid map and what a raid brings home (`yardRaidHome`, `UI.haul`); its names start `yard`/`YARD_`, and it keeps its panel styles in its own `<style>`. `life.js`: birds, distant ships, fireflies, mine dust.
  - The story (`docs/story.md`). `story.js`: act one's chapters, the engine (scenes, visitors, goals, saving all of the story's state) and the journal (story, the cast, days and mood, codex). `acttwo.js`: act two's six chapters, its new faces and codex pages, the Long Bright Night (`storyNight`, used by `planNight`), the storm season, the Exchange's crews, the tallies the story counts from (`ACT.tally`), and the two ticks: `lifeTick` (game time, from `main.js`) and `actFrame` (every frame, from `storyTick`). `quests.js`: the cast's own stories (`QUESTS`, offered at the Keep; `castGift` for gifts that grow when a story is done). `events.js`: the day events (`EVENTS`), their cards and the journal's days. `mood.js`: the claim's mood (`MOOD`, `moodAdd`), its HUD face, and the wrap of `Villager.eff` that lets mood, festivals and fevers change work speed.
  - Loot: `loot.js` is the Armory's data (`D`: items, traits, modules, Lark parts, relics, drops, `WORKSHOP` costs and times, `ATTUNE`, `CELLS`) and engine (`createLoot`). `gear.js`: gear on people (`itemStats`, `dpsOf`, `fightOf`, overhauls, cells), the stash (`addToStash`) and the economy. `bench.js`: Odile's bench at the Lens Workshop (salvage, retune, overhaul, cells, attune, assay, blueprints), its jobs (`GAME.bench`, game time, one at a time; better with a good Engineering hand) and its panel. `fittings.js`: modules in building sockets and the Lark's parts (`GAME.sockets`, `modV(b, stat)`, `larkV`), what they do each frame (`fitTick`), their props on the buildings, the sockets block, the fit picker, the Lark's panel and the courier at the mast. `finds.js`: the rare-find moment (`lootMoment`), the item panel with compare, the stash's sort and filter, and "best for this job". The bench and fittings save as `workshop` in the claim's save.
  - Sound (no sound files: all synthesised live with Web Audio). The music is written as notes in `score.js` (each theme's chords, tunes, drum patterns and form; the notation is explained at its top, and every theme grows from one motif). `music.js` plays it, choosing the theme from the game's state and changing on bar lines, and runs the Music box in the settings. `audio.js` has the instruments, effects and ambience: `AUDIO.play(name, position)` plays an effect (unknown names are ignored; a position makes it quieter with distance), `AUDIO.loot(rarity)`, `AUDIO.rain(0..1)`.
  - `wildlight.js`: the Wildlight (`WL_KINDS`: Glimmer, Flarehound, Lodestar, Brightmaw), their climb up the cliff (`climbRoute`), hunting, and the turrets, the Lark and the watch.
  - `nights.js`: the shape of a night (`NIGHT`, `planNight`): the lookout's afternoon warning, the dusk banner, waves up their own cliffs, Bright Nights and their bosses (`BOSSES`), the boss bar, harm you can see (wall cracks and rubble, snuffed lamps, dimmed yards, the hurt limping home), the watch mending the wall by day, muzzle flashes and camera shake, and the dawn report.
  - `weather.js`: the day's weather and tomorrow's forecast (`WEATHER`, `forceWeather`), its moods by day and night (`weatherMood`, called from `applyTimeOfDay`), rain, fog, the cloud deck, puddles, lightning, the Lightning Rod building, and the weather in the clock.
  - The Keep, cut open (Fallout Shelter's other side). `keep.js`: the rules (the five studies `STUDY`, room costs and seats, `startStudy` / `endStudy`, time away), the Keep view's own pan and zoom (`KCAM`, `frameKeep`, `keepFocus`) and tapping (`pickKeep`). `keeplook.js`: how it looks: the shell, the switchback stair, the attic, each room furnished by kind and level (`KFURN`; the Hearth Hall, the Dormitory and the attic by the Keep's level), windows that show the sky and let the day in, flames, the notice board drawn from the journal, the empty room's Furnish sign, a room fitted out in front of you (`KEEPV.fit`), and lamplight faked in the shaders like the mine (`KGLOW`). Static pieces are baked into one mesh per material with vertex-colour tints (`kput`, `kmerge`). Rooms add the places people use (`kspot`: study seats, table seats, beds, favourite corners). `keeplife.js`: who is where indoors: everyone's place by the hour (`keepWant`, `keepAssign`), walking the stair and the ladder (`kpath`), sitting, lying under their own quilt, the founders' and visitors' favourite rooms (`KFAV`), resting indoors by day (`keepRestPlan`, from `think`), and Tansy the cat and Brindle the dog. None of it is saved; `keepTick(seconds)` runs it without drawing for tests.
  - The front door. `title.js`: the loading screen (`loadStep`, filled by `boot` in `main.js`), the title screen (`TITLE`, `titleShow`; the claim behind it at golden hour with the camera drifting, from `frame`; on a new device the starting kit is set out for show and taken away again by a new claim), the sheets over everything (Claims, About, New claim, carrying a claim off as a code and loading one), and the short first look at how the claim works (`introShow`). `begin(fresh)` in `main.js` goes from the title into the claim. `settings.js`: the Settings panel and what it keeps under `bv-settings` (text size, camera speed, less motion, autosave, speed limits, tips; the picture's detail stays under `bv-quality`, the volumes are `AUDIO`'s). `hints.js`: the first-time tips (`HINT`, shown once each per device, remembered under `bv-hints`; they watch the game from `hintsTick` rather than being called). Toasts (`toast` in `ui.js`) wait in a queue: at most three on screen (two on a phone), low above the controls or a phone's panel (one at a time just under the top bar when a phone's panel leaves no room above it), held out of sight while a card is up (a scene, a day event's card, the dawn report, the settings and their sheets).
  - Saves (`saves.js`): three claims, one per slot. Slot 1 is stored under the key the game always used (`brightvein-keep-v1`), so a claim from before slots is claim 1; slots 2 and 3 are `brightvein-keep-v1-s2` and `-s3`. `bv-slots` keeps which slot is open and the names the player gave them. `saveGame(force)` and `loadSave()` (in `main.js`) work on the open slot; with autosave off only a forced save is made. Opening another claim, or starting a new one over a claim that's on the page, reloads the page (`reloadInto(slot, 'continue' | 'new')`; a note in the tab's session says to go straight in). A claim travels as a code: `BV1z.` + the save deflated and in base64url + `.` + a CRC-32 check (`exportCode`, `importCode`; `z` deflate-raw, `g` gzip, `p` plain for browsers without `CompressionStream`). A damaged code fails its check and is refused with a plain reason (`codeWhy`). A save carries its claim's `name`. A save is mended as it's opened or imported (`safeMend`: numbers that aren't numbers take their starting values, a person without `stats` is left out, a missing look is drawn afresh); when something is left out, or a claim still won't open, the save as it was is kept under `<slot key>-broken-<time>` (`safeBackup`), and a claim that won't open reloads the page to the title with three ways on (`safeBroken`, `safeBrokenSheet`). `saveGame` builds the save part by part (`safeSave`): a part that fails keeps its last good copy, and no NaN is ever saved. `frame()` runs its systems in guarded groups; a fault anywhere is logged once and shown once as a gentle toast (`safeFault`), and lands in `__bv.SAFE.faults`, which the smoke test checks.
  - Keeping the draw count down (phones use Light): each building's still pieces are merged into one mesh per material when it's built (`bake` in `kit.js`), and pieces that move as one (a turret's head and its tilting stock) are merged within themselves when their group is marked `userData.rigid`. Those per-building meshes are merged again across the whole town, one mesh per material (`townMerge` in `kit.js`, run before each frame is drawn; remade when a building is put up, raised, moved or taken down, and off in build mode): the buildings keep their own meshes for tapping, on `PICK_LAYER`, which the raycaster sees and the cameras don't. Everyone is drawn in one instanced draw per view from one atlas of their sheets (`crowd.js`: their sprites stay and are still set as before, on a layer the cameras don't draw); their ground shadows are one more (`shadowsDraw` in `people.js`); the cloud puffs round the plateau are one mesh (`buildClouds` in `world.js`). On Light there's no post-processing, the sun's shadow map is 1024 and redrawn every third frame (`frame` in `main.js`), turning turret heads cast no shadow (`lightCasts` in `kit.js`), and fewer particles live at once (`FX_CAP` in `kit.js`). Anything taken out of the scene for good frees its geometry on the graphics card (`safeFree` in `kit.js`; a rebuilt dig, a building taken down, a guest who leaves). The Keep and the mine are drawn once behind the loading screen (`warmViews` in `main.js`) so their shaders are ready before anyone looks in. `python3 test/perf.py [w h dpr]` grows a big claim and prints each detail level's draws and frame time in each view; `__bv.drawCalls()` counts a frame's draws; `__bv.drawOnly()` draws one as the game does.
  - The cast are guests: only settlers count against the Keep's beds (`bedsUsed()`, `guestCount()` in `people.js`), and indoors a guest takes a bunk only when one is spare, otherwise a bedroll by the hall's fire (`keepAssign` in `keeplife.js`). The people pill shows settlers / beds, and the guests as +n.
  - `mine.js`: the dig, Fallout Shelter's side: a grid of rooms in the cut face (`MINE` in `core.js`: 6 rows, 10 columns west of the shaft), digging, merging (2 or 3 wide), room levels, the kinds of room and what they do, the lift cage, trouble down the mine (cave-ins, floods, gas, Wildlight breaking out), the Mine view's own pan and zoom, and the dig's panels. Rooms are pseudo-buildings with ids `m:row.col` (`d:row.col` while being dug), so jobs work as on buildings. `minelook.js`: how the dig looks: the rock front with a hole for every room, rooms dressed by row and level and furnished by kind, lamplight faked in the shaders (no extra lights), swaying lanterns, carts, the cage, visible trouble.
- `src/sky/`, `src/ship/`, `src/ships/`: the skies, the ship builder and the six ships, from the airship game. Ordinary ES modules.
  - `voyage.js`: `makeSky` (open, sail, voyages, the HUD, hit feedback; ships built per refit with their own paint, `shipNow`, `loadoutOf`). `progress.js`: the fleet's save (v2: ships with refits, paint, wear and repairs; the crew; raid records; the bounty) and `migrate` for older fleets. `refit.js`: the Shipyard's rules: prices and what opens each ship (`SHIPYARD`, `lockedWhy`), refits by part (`PARTS`, `refitCost`, `recipeFor`: how a refit changes the ship's recipe), looks (`PAINTS`, `SAILS`, `FIGURES`, `applyPaint`, `figurehead`), the crew's effects (`crewEffects`), `loadout`, and repairs. `port.js`: the Shipyard screen (Ship, Refit and Looks tabs) and choosing the skies.
  - `raids.js`: a raid from setting out to its colours coming down: each kind's objective and set piece, the bosses (`BOSSES`: the Black Candle, the Grindstone), the storm's weather, and `info()` for the HUD. `raiders.js`: the raiders (and friendly barges), their colours and boss hooks. `effects.js`: smoke, debris, flashes, burning wrecks.
- `src/shell.html`, `src/style.css`: the page and its look.
- `assets/claim/`: the claim's painted textures (from Briarwatch). `assets/map/`, `assets/ships/`, `assets/fonts/`: from the airship game.
- `assets/cast/`, `assets/portraits/`, `assets/items/`, `assets/events/`, `assets/skies/`: pictures shown as they are (portraits on cards and in scenes, item pictures, the day events' cards, the places on the raid map), inlined by the build as `ART.cast`, `ART.portraits`, `ART.items`, `ART.events`, `ART.skies`, named by id. Shrink portraits to 256 × 256, items to 128 × 128 and event and place pictures to 480 × 360 (webp) before adding them (`tools/add-art.py`: portraits and items at quality 75, the cast's pixel art lossless). Re-encode from Chris's full-size originals, never from a webp already in `assets/`. The page has a 16 MB limit (about 11.9 MB now).
- `src/sky/sites.js`: the places to raid (`SITES`: Tallow's four, act two's two, the Brass Road convoy and the storm front, with their objectives, stars and pay), their models, the wanted captains (`makeBounty`), and `REPEAT` (a place raided again pays half). `docs/story.md` has the table.
- `reference/`: pictures to draw from; never loaded by the game.

## Checking a change

```
npm install
node tools/build.mjs            # dist/brightvein.html (open it) and dist/brightvein.artifact.html (publish it)
python3 test/smoke.py           # plays the claim and a voyage headless; must end with "all good"
python3 test/raid.py citadel brig fight fair 2   # flies a raid with the autopilot (site, ship, strike|fight, skies, refit level)
python3 test/perf.py 390 844 3  # a big claim's draw calls and frame times at each detail level (here at a phone's size)
python3 test/shot.py out.png 1280 800 "<js to run>" 6000 3000   # a screenshot after running some JS
node test/music.mjs             # the written music: every bar adds up, tunes sit on their chords, notes in range
python3 test/audio.py [--wav]   # renders every theme, effect and ambience offline and measures it (--wav: shots/audio/)
```

- `window.__bv` exposes the game for tests (`simulate(seconds)` runs the claim without drawing). The page opens on the title screen; tests wait for `#loading` to be hidden, then call `__bv.begin(true)` (a new claim) or `__bv.begin(false)` (the saved one). `THREE` isn't on `__bv` (so the build can trim three.js); make a vector with `__bv.camera.position.clone().set(x, y, z)`. `test/saves/`: real saves from older builds, which the smoke test opens.
- Headless rendering is software (SwiftShader) and slow: test logic with `simulate`, and take few screenshots.
- The game is published at the Brightvein artifact link (see `README.md`). After a change, build, then publish `dist/brightvein.artifact.html` to that link (pass the url) so Chris's link keeps working. The artifact declares the `downloads` capability (saving a claim as a file asks the viewer through it, in `saves.js`); leave `capabilities` out when republishing so it carries over.
